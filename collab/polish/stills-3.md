## Four rulings of the judge's, for the product owner to uphold or not

1. **A cell passes only if whatever missed before is in a picture again, and only on pictures that can ask all of it.** A cell is as good as its worst kind of words; a kind that missed and is in no newer picture leaves the cell unseen, whatever has landed since and however well the rest reads. (The second round let two misses go unseen without a word; `stills-2.md` says so now.)
2. **A legend that alone says what the signs mean is a word one must read to act**, as a placeholder that is its field's only label is: 4.5.
3. **What is drawn in macOS's own colours for that very thing is macOS's, whoever fills it.** White on the system's colour for a selected row, nothing thinned: 4.58 passes S6. (No picture shows a list of macOS's own in that state to hold it against: the ruling rests on the colour's name in the source.) The side of an ordinary titled window is macOS's too, so the settings window's S6 is asked of ink alone, and passes. So is the ground of a standard menu: with Reduce Transparency it differs by 11 levels in the light look and 23 in the dark with what lies behind, over S7's mark of 2, and costs nothing.
4. The foot of the shortcuts' sheet, "Press any key to close" (4.09 in the dark look over white), is secondary: any key closes it unread.

**A question that waits for a picture.** By the source the coach and Check Setup are ordinary titled windows with no material, so their four cells "on glass" would have nothing to ask, and the rubric asks no cell "where a panel has no ground to see through". No picture shows it yet: with the glass on there is only Check Setup alone, whose ground reads 241,243,244 against 246 with the glass off. Both windows over two strong colours with the glass on would settle it. Until then the four are counted, as unseen.
